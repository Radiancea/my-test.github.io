Scaling Generative Models for Procedural 3D Worlds

April 15, 2026

We present a novel approach to generating infinite procedural 3D environments using diffusion-based architectures trained on massive synthetic datasets.

Overview

Procedural generation has long been a cornerstone of game development and simulation, but traditional methods rely on hand-crafted rules and heuristics. Our approach leverages large-scale generative models to learn the underlying distribution of 3D environments, enabling the synthesis of diverse, coherent worlds from a single text prompt.

Figure 1. Architecture overview of the procedural generation pipeline.

Method

Our method consists of three stages: (1) a voxel-based scene encoder that compresses 3D environments into latent representations, (2) a conditional diffusion model that generates scene layouts from text descriptions, and (3) a neural renderer that converts generated layouts into high-fidelity meshes with PBR materials.

The scene encoder is trained on a curated dataset of 500K procedurally generated environments spanning indoor rooms, outdoor landscapes, and urban scenes. We use a hierarchical VQ-VAE architecture to capture both local geometric details and global scene structure.

Results

Quantitative evaluations on our held-out test set demonstrate that our model achieves an FID score of 12.3 on generated scene renders, significantly outperforming the previous state-of-the-art (FID 28.7). Human evaluators rated our generated environments as "realistic and coherent" in 87% of cases, compared to 52% for baseline approaches.

We also demonstrate zero-shot generalization to novel scene types not seen during training, including underwater environments and alien landscapes. The model successfully transfers learned structural priors to these unseen domains.

Conclusion

We have shown that large-scale generative models can effectively learn to produce diverse, high-quality 3D environments from text descriptions. Our approach opens new possibilities for automated content creation in games, simulations, and virtual reality applications.
